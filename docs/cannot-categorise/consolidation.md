<h1>Consolidation</h1>

> **Context**: [Philosophy V3](/docs/philosophy-v3/)

---

The provenance of value is unchosen, i.e. the motive-force underlying your core values are, at some point, not chosen but just present. Committing to a certain kind of life is not invalidated by how it compares with other kinds of life, for a comparison always needs a shared standard, and the standards for the "good" or the "ideal" may not be shared.

My values are, at their core, the emergent property of my core composition. I do not feel any inherent obligation or even any inherent value for my life - life is optional, its value is contingent on my experience of it. And because my experience of life is individuated, so are my values, i.e. the locus of valuing is a single site, not a distributed one. As for virtues, to me, any virtues - insofar as they ARE virtues - are instrumental, not identifying. I do not loathe myself. I do not love myself. I just am myself.

What I am seeking is a mechanism by which I can sustain my life more reliably, because I happen to want to live, I fear hardship and the deterioration of will, and I desire comfort and power (though not as permanent states but as tools in my grasp, to be used when I need). By seeking a "why", I am seeking what I think is such a mechanism. The "why", ironically, is instrumental.

> Importantly, the "why" is not about belief, but practice.
