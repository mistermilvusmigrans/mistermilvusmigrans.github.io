<h1>On Women</h1>

> And humanity.

---

I do not stand apart from history, neither do my ideas, values, and visions. Philosophy, to me, is not just a domain of abstractions that transcend the particularities of our time. Abstractions are about particularities, drawn from them and addressing them. With this in mind, I seek to speak about women.

---

They have stood as a half humanity from when humanity arose. They were not created from man's rib, nor as an afterthought by the gods so man can have company and comfort. They were there and partook, when stones were first sharpened, when the spears were first thrown, when the sticks were first draped with hide, when the stars were first admired. They were there and partook, when the seeds were first sown; perhaps they sowed it first themselves. They were there and partook, when the first harvest was gathered, the first meal made, first fibres woven, the first clothes worn. They were there and partook, when the bricks and the roads were first laid, when spirits were first whispered to in bargains, when the first blow of war bloodied the pages of history.

They were not merely watching, not merely kept, even under the laws that made them objects to keep. They weren't and couldn't be, for they were human, one half of humanity, always present, always working, dreaming, despairing, and dancing. They were not a compromise of humanity, no matter the words of the priests and philosophers, because they ARE humanity, one half of it, and always had been.

Sidelined, they worked.

Subjugated, they worked.

Silenced, they worked.

They worked, dreamed, despaired and danced, a partner in nature even if not in law. And when law began to catch up, when they gained a voice and a will, all we saw was all that had ever been: humanity. Not goddesses, not angels, not demons nor witches. Just... humanity, not restored, not retrieved, but recognised.

If I were to represent humanity, I would do so with woman, not because she is more human, but because seeing her as humans was the achievement it should never have been. Even as women's bodies adjusted and accommodated for the rigours of reproduction, even as they paid the price not in their power but in the imbalance of power against the power-hungry, they were always theirs by rights, doing so much, struggling often, ignored as often, but always theirs, always surviving, thriving, suffering, and surrendering to death, with the strength and the fragility that was humanity's.
