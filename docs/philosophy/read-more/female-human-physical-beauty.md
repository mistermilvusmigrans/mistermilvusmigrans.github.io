# The meaning of beauty
Beauty is the identification of one's psychological response to values and virtues we experience as embodied in an object. Identification is key: a cat can have its own psychological response to the values and virtues it experiences embodied in a human, such as security, comfort and confidence, but it would not find it "beautiful", because "beauty" is a conceptualisation of experience, not an experience itself. Why is this distinction between experience and the conceptualisation of experience important? Because it distinguishes beauty from a subjective appreciation to an appreciation that is metaphysically grounded, i.e. grounded in one's premises, especially one's premises about reality and about what is "valuable" within it. If one's metaphysics are objective, i.e. based on the recognition of that which corresponds to reality, then beauty is as much an objective appreciation than a subjective one.

# The relevance of beauty
Why do I elaborate the meaning of beauty? Because a human being is a conceptual being, i.e. a being that grasps reality not just from experiences but from an integration of these experiences into the broader context of his/her life. To illustrate, consider the idea of a "diet". It is not merely about pursuing food that feels good, but that feels good in the long-run, that enables the physical and psychological well-being that ensures and extends one's ability to "feel good". Animals cannot plan a diet, they cannot choose to optimise their food for health and/or taste; humans can, because a human grasps abstractions, i.e. not just what is, but what can be, as per the essence of an object's nature and potential for action. As a being with a mind that is geared toward integration, and that cannot sustain its effort without some form of self-driven focus, a human does not have a choice about whether to have premises for one's values; the only choice is about *what* premises to hold. Thus, beauty is at once a concretisation and an affirmation of one's premises, which means it is what concretely inspires and/or motivates a human toward the further affirmation of one's premises, in thought and, importantly, in action. Let me sharpen this idea with an example. If a human values vitality, he is inspired by a vision of a verdant forest; if he/she sees vitality as doomed, sees reality as wretched and bound toward wickedness, and instead, values acceptance, a verdant forest would be at best a fleeting fancy, and at worst a mockery. However, the image of a decaying, desolate graveyard would resonate with them by affirming their views and inspiring (if you get my meaning) their value for acceptance. To them, the image would say, "Here is reality as you know it to be, accept it as you must." Thus, validating and living by one's views of beauty is as important as validating and living by one's values, as per the same reasoning: premises shape your responses, which shape your motivations, which shape the actions you consistently do or tend to do, which shape your life and the character that enables it or disables it. This is why elaborating on the meaning of beauty is not just valuable, but vital.

# The relevance of human beauty
Let us speak now of the category of beings in this world that, as a category, are more valuable to me than anything else in this world: humans. Why? Because of their potential to *create* value long-range, sustain life at a scale far beyond the greatest of beasts. They create value, not from nothing, but from things in this world and their potentials; hence, do not take my meaning to be: "humans matter more than the world," as that would be absurd. No, humans matter because the world matters absolutely. Now, humans are embodied beings — not a "spirit" in a shell of matter, not a shell of matter without spirit, but matter imbued with spirit, ignited by it, in a sense. As humans are of such value to me, and as they are embodied beings, my value for them is concretised and affirmed not merely by "internal" qualities (intelligence, determination, etc.) and not merely by "external" qualities (strength, agility, etc.); no, my value for them is concretised and affirmed by the way these qualities integrate as a totality.

Now, I must make my meaning clear. I do not mean I disregard the value of those who are weak, cumbersome, unintelligent, despairing and so on (in one or more respects). I rather mean that I can learn to regard their value and their potential for value by means of someone strong, agile, intelligent, determined, etc. Consider it in this way: a disabled man has a strength of his own, physical and mental (if he did not, he would be a vegetable), and he has the potential any human has, i.e. the potential to choose vitality within his capacity. But what stands out to me is his disability and the difficulties he faces due to it. However, if I see a human as strong in a more clear, concrete way, I can learn to associate "human" with such strength, and as I integrate this association, by ability for abstraction can then learn to apply this association to a disabled man as well. So, to be clear, when I speak of my value for human beauty, I speak of my value for concretising and affirming the human potential, and I speak of being able to tie my premises about the value of a human to a concretisation I can grasp with clarity and apply with surety.

# Introspection about female human beauty
Now, I shall take a turn toward something more subjective, not to relish in it but to return with clarity to objective premises. I can grasp male physical beauty with clarity, since a man's body is, for the most part, streamlined toward physical efficacy ("streamlined", not "maximised"; I speak here of the clarity rather than the quantity of capacity, for in quantity, a leopard or a wolf are far beyond many physical capacities of a human). However, I always found female physical beauty to be more complex, which is curious, because my attraction (physical and psychological) to women is far more intense than it is to men. The complexity has nothing to do with physical efficacy, as physical efficacy is a capacity of a healthy human as such, not a man per se. Rather, the complexity arises due to the things that seem to undermine a woman's physical efficacy: lower propensity for building muscle, lower muscle mass and bone density, breast-related issues, the side-effects of menopause, etc. The complexity is that the very causes that lead to this undermining are the things that either give a woman her "womanly" attractive qualities: softer and smooth contours due to more subcutaneous fat (which is tied to the same causes that lower their propensity for building muscle, and that lower the muscle mass and bone density they can achieve), breasts (they are beautiful to me); the physiological processes that give rise to these qualities also have a pattern (menstrual cycles) that ends with menopause.

## The quality of yielding
However, in trying to seek the essence of my attraction to women, I think I have my answer. Let me elaborate. Firstly, I abstracted the quality of a "yielding-nature" as a key aspect of my attraction, by which I mean a physical "receptivity" (e.g. softness, sensitivity, etc.). Why? Consider: what is one thing that makes a woman's form, anatomically, distinct from a man's form? (1) Smoother, softer contours and the flesh that shapes them, and (2) a woman's sex/sensual organs. To elaborate on (2), consider that the vagina is, in many ways, optimised to enhance the experience of reception, both for the receiver (the woman) and the one giving her the experience (another man or woman); it is self-cleaning, soft, sensitive, and lubricates itself upon arousal, etc. Also consider a woman's breasts, which are soft yet sensitive to pleasure and pain (pleasure being more relevant to this review). Let me be precise here: I do not mean a "yielding-nature" must be maximised for maximal attractiveness; this is absurd. A human being is an integration, not an isolation, and thus, this quality must integrate with the vital and valuable physical qualities of a human being, such as physical efficacy. Hence, there is a range of quantities in which this quality becomes attractive (from softer/rounder but healthy women, to sharper, more well-defined and strong women who do not obsess with definition), and also a range of quantities in which it becomes unattractive: (a) too little, where we get a woman who obsessively sought definition over health (crucially, this obsession is a turn-off for me with respect to men as well, which is why most bodybuilders are not attractive to me), and (b) too much, where we get a woman who is feeble/fragile. To put it in a phrase: delicate features are attractive when they are a part of a robust frame, and not in and of themselves. Note that this applies to men and to women, but for me, with men, the range of quantities for this "yielding-nature" is at the higher end, whereas with women, this range is closer to the lower end. Why? Because, as far as I grasp it, this "yielding-nature" is a virtue when integrated with core virtues, because at its best, it is receptivity, sensitivity and fluidity (not fluidity in principles but in implementation), but for men, due to their distinctive physical potential, I lean toward less of it (not none), whereas for women, due to their distinctive physical potential, I lean toward more of it. Also, not that I speak of a "yielding-nature" as a physical rather than psychological quality. That said, I do think it also applies as a psychological quality, yet not in the sense of submissive per se (submissive in the right context, perhaps, but not as a constant). Indeed, based on my own attractions, I find that the range of psychological "yielding-ness" that I find attractive is as wide or perhaps wider, which is why I find both more soft-natured women and more bold/assertive women attractive (as an aside, note that assertiveness is important for anyone, but the way someone integrates it into their life can vary in expression).

That said, a woman's beauty is scarcely diminished if this faculty is degraded or destroyed in her; her form and the core of her human soul together are a wonder in themselves. I would say that the same goes for any of a woman's womanly aspects: malice, misfortune or something else may take it away from her, yet she is, first and foremost, a human, her human potential brought out through first and foremost through her individuality than her femininity. I say this because when I speak of a woman's beauty and my adoration for it, it is to concretise and affirm the values and virtues that are deeply distinct in a beautiful woman, but that can be embodied by any human  (and should be, as integrated with one's identity).

## Powerful but painful potential
Lastly, I shall speak of the most painful part of my introspection. A woman's distinct womanliness (in the way that attracts me) arises from the same causes that also cause women pain and potential harm: menstrual cycles, lower physical potential for strength and agility, menopause, breast cancer, etc. A woman's body, in many ways, develops in anticipation for child-bearing and child-birth. In this anticipation, her body develops a form of human beauty that is sensual yet strong, tender and yet capable of toughness. However, this anticipation is a biological fact, but not an imperative, biological or moral, because a woman is not first a woman but first a human, and a human being is, at his/her core, a volitional being. This anticipation may be for nought, either by choice or by compulsion, but what it brings out is womanly beauty and nothing less. That said, this anticipation brings with it hurt, the potential for harm, and a potentially trying time in the future, when this anticipation is let go forever. A woman's beauty, thus, arises from a body realising (or trying to realise) a powerful potential, but this process is intensely exacting precisely due to its immensity. Some regard this as a handicap, a curse of sorts. Some regard it as a fact of life, not a condemnation but not something to cure either. I say, however, that we cannot ask nature for perfection, but we can ask it from a human, who strives and strives toward value and virtue. Through a woman's form, nature has brought forth a precious form of beauty, but I think it is up to humans to not only cherish this precious form but fortify it, with worldly wisdom and science. A woman's form is a human form with the human potential for strength in life and solidarity with life, yet it brings out this potential not by streamlining physical efficacy but by effusing it with a yielding-nature and a deeply personal decorativeness.

# Man and woman as embodied forms of human beauty
If I were asked to present humanity practically, I would present a man. If I were asked to present humanity philosophically, I would present a woman. Because while both are expressions of human potential, a man's form is streamlined for efficacy, whereas a woman's form is an integration of efficacy with a deeply personal decorativeness that belies a powerful yet painful potential; the former highlights functional liveliness, the latter highlights lively functionality. Man is a human stripped to essentials. Woman is a human expanded to through essentials. Both embodied virtues that are essentially human, best embodied by the forms but not tied to them. Now, note that philosophy is intimately tied to practicality, since philosophy is what shapes what practicality is, and practicality is what realises philosophy. Hence, to embody philosophy is to embody practicality and vice versa, but the distinction lies in the direction of focus. Not also that this "embodiment" is through physical form, not the psychological or philosophical function of a human being. Human beauty in every form is deeply philosophical and practical, but I think that a male body's beauty can be better grasped by moving from "practical" to "philosophical", and a female body's beauty can be better grasped by moving from "philosophical" to "practical". But I grant that there is no separation, ultimately.
