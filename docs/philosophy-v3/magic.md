# What kind of document this is
This is not an argument, let alone a proof. It is a way of organising our perception of the world and our lives in it. I will be redefining words in ways that may be contentious, but I think there is value to what this document is trying to do: imbue the DESCRIPTIVE - facts, descriptions, definitions, concepts, conclusions, etc. - with SUBJECTIVE FUEL that sustains our experience of life, in the moment and (hopefully) in the future.

# Defining "magic"
To begin with, let me state that I am, philosophically, a naturalist. This is important, because I shall not depart from this philosophical position when defining magic. Now, I am not defining magic as a separate force or phenomenon. Rather, magic is an aspect of what already exists, which may be labelled under different names in different contexts. To me, magic is a type of subjectively-driven interaction with something arising from integration, such that the integrated whole is more than the sum of its parts, i.e. something with properties that arise not from being contained in its parts nor from the aggregation of the properties of its parts, but from the parts coming together in the particular way they do. In other words, it is a type of subjectively-driven interaction with emergence, which is a class of phenomena that exists abundantly in the world, from chemicals and compounds to life to concrete to consciousness.

Firstly, what does "subjectively-driven" mean? Here, it means something - an act or a practice - whose basis is a set of affective and/or intuitive premises, i.e. based on subjective affect/intuition. This is not to say there can be no logic or reasoning involved; rather, the point is that any logic or reasoning builds on this affective/intuitive basis.

Secondly, what type of subjectively-driven interaction is magic? It is the type that seeks to shape outcomes with intention. Now, if it is subjectively-driven, we can reasonably expect the domain of magic's outcome to be primarily the shaping of lived experience and orientation, which is powerful but also more tightly-scoped than many common uses of the word "magic".

Now, why call it magic? Magic is a loaded term, and while that may seem like a reason to NOT use it in this context, that is precisely the reason I have used it in this context. One part of my reason is that the word "magic" evokes the idea of controlling chaos, i.e. controlling something you do not fully understand in a way you do not fully understand, but doing it nonetheless, because it works for you or - to your knowledge/belief - may work for you. A lot of life feels this way, especially when dealing with the complex and often mysterious domain that is our "self" - the body, the mind, and the various ways they integrate... from philosophical premises to psychological tendencies to physiological shifts that feed back into psychology, and psychology that feeds back into philosophy, and so on and so on... all this coming together, whether we are aware of it or not - and much of it we are NOT aware of - in ways that are hard to predict, control, or coordinate. "Magic", thus, feels like a fitting way to describe the process of trying to shape our lived experience and orientation when we do not fully understand both the domain and the method.

The other part of my reason is that, even when the domain or the method or both are well-understood, "magic" evokes the affective part of the domain/method in a way other descriptions may not, at least not as effectively. Magic becomes a way to turn understanding into subjective fuel, making understanding emotionally usable, as a means for grounding oneself, firing oneself up, focusing oneself, etc.

# Magic, science, and technology
To put it simple: science is the practice of seeking knowledge, technology is the practice making knowledge practically and consistently useful, and magic is the practice of making knowledge and technology subjectively usable. The use of words heal and teach and torture even as they merely enter your ears as sounds, the use of movements alter your orientation to existence without arguing you into it, the use of rituals to keep tying your lived experience to broader truths and values... all these, to me, are forms of magic.
