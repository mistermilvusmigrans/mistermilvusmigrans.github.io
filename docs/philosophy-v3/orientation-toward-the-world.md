# What "orientation" means here
Orientation consists of the answers to two questions:

1. What are the principles of existence qua existence?
2. How does reality relate to you? This involves:
   1. Its laws
   2. Its contents

My answer to question 1 is here:

[Metaphysics, Philosophy V1](/docs/philosophy-v1/metaphysics/)

In this document, I shall attempt to answer question 2.

# Purpose
The universe as such has no purpose, no intent; the only intent there is comes from the living. The living are a part of the universe, and through them does intent arise within the universe. Intent is real, it is just not in the space and the stars. The universe is what it is, regardless of our needs, desires, and prayers. It as a whole does not "value" or "strive toward" anything; it just is, and things in it do what they do. Whether that turns out to be good for you lies in the outcome, not any intent or design or destiny.

# Favour
The universe is largely inhospitable to us. Most of its space is unlivable for even a few seconds, and much of this planet that we consider our home has no designs to protect the life that resides on it. Far from protect, it is deadly, for the most part, even when it is at its safest. The weather wears down even mountains, let alone life-forms that must keep seeking shelter from it. Consider storms, eruptions, earthquakes. Consider pathogens, parasites, predators. Consider that this world has killed far more species than it has kept, and consider that there is no guarantee, no matter how much we secure or strengthen ourselves, that you or I shall see tomorrow.

Among humans, who are a part of this world, we find as many foes as friends, as much pain as joy, if not more, far more, often. Consider war, coercion, control, abuse. Consider the loved ones you may not see again, tomorrow or ten years from now. Consider the wonderful people who are no more favoured by fortune than the vile and vicious.

This is not a world for the living.

It is just the world, and we live in it.

# Conclusion
This is more of affect than argument, so take it as such. When I think of the world, I do not find it wonderful or beautiful or worthy of any worship or adoration. It is, to me, a horrific world, often, terrible in its potentials. And it is my world, and I want to live in it and make my way through it, not because of what it is, but because it is.
