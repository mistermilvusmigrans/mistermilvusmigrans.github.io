<h1>Staking Your Claim On Your Life</h1>

---

I posit this: before you choose what bests serves your interests, you should have decided what your core interests are, and the decisions you make regarding these are made by committing to what you must - MUST, without exception - gain and/or keep, what you would prefer to gain and/or keep (all else equal), and what you are willing to lose, even if you lose it/them forever.

Let us concretise before we generalise. Consider the case of a life-partnership. If you are willing to commit to it even if it were arranged, or if it were done with a partner you did not strongly align with in some way that was important to you (e.g. with respect to values, disposition, life-goals, etc.), then your interests may be shaped like this: strong preference for a life-partner, willing to lose one or more aspects of deep/intimate compatibility... perhaps with the non-negotiable being HAVING a life-partner at some point. However, if you are unwilling to give up these aspects of deep/intimate compatibility, you are not necessarily being dense or inordinately obstinate per se (you might be, I don't know you, but as such, you're not necessarily so). You are not necessarily "sacrificing" many potential life-partnerships. The reason? Because your decision about what you are unwilling to lose is what shapes your interests. A life-partnership that goes against this decision may NOT BE in your interest to begin with. You cannot sacrifice something you do not value in the first place. Neither way is "worse" or "better" in and of itself (only by a standard you've committed to), each is just what it is.

The idea same applies someone who is willing to die for a certain way of life, or certain freedoms. They are not being stupid or "impractical" (at least, not necessarily), because practicality depends on what you want to achieve, and by committing to certain non-negotiables, they are staking a claim on the kind of life they fundamentally care about. Losing their lives in the pursuit of fighting for their way of life or their freedoms may NOT BE a loss, because a life where they shied away from such a fight may be against their interests to begin with. They pursue the fight as an integral part of their interests. On the flip side, someone who is willing to compromise their way of life or freedoms is staking a claim on a kind of life that prefers existence over this particular form of existence (if a choice must be made). Neither way is "worse" or "better" in and of itself (only by a standard you've committed to), each is just what it is.

> The core idea is that committing to a certain kind of life is not necessarily invalidated by how it compares with other kinds of life, for a comparison always needs a shared standard, and the standards for the "good" or the "ideal" may not be shared.
