# Seeking the truth from "Foundations"
Consider: [*Foundations*, **Philosophy V3**](./foundations.md)

Why do I seek all this? These truths - insofar as they are truths - these frameworks, these thoughts and hypotheses? Perhaps it's some innate or absorbed or adopted desire for the truth. I tried to build a moral framework out of this desire, once. But I think that I seek all this because I want to carry myself to the next moment, and the next, and the next, maybe out of compulsion or fear or the closest thing I have to meaning. And to do so, I want to understand how to bear my weight and the weight of that which I take with me, accumulate, absorb, adopt. Thus, I seek to understand the mechanisms, internal and external, that play a role in my journey through the world as the world confronts me.

# The self-referential loop
In this vein, I want to address something that has been weighing on me for many, many years, almost as long as I can remember: the self-referential loop. I suppose this is an offshoot of the same faculties and mechanisms that allow me to reflect, examine my own mind's contents and abstract from them. First, let me define the term. "Self-referential loop" refers to an ongoing mental process or framework to refer to myself as a separate entity, I would say in 3rd person. How this often manifests is in the form of thinking about how others think of you, thinking about your life as a narrative centered around you, thinking about your "legacy" or "image" (private or public).

# Merits and demerits
Now, certain forms can be beneficial. For example, understanding (or trying to understand) how others perceive you in a certain context (public, professional, etc.) can help you orient yourself toward your best interests in this context (e.g. maintaining a non-criminal public image, maintaining trust and reliability professionally... etc.). Moreover, per my observations, a narrative self-referential loop can help reflect on, share and clarify your life and decisions and journey.

The key point, however, is that the self-referential loop (SRL) is a process or a framework or a tool; there is no reason for it to be the dominant framework with which one orients oneself to the world. In fact, I'd argue that the SRL is profoundly unfit for general/broad application for 2 main reasons: (1) epistemic sparsity and (2) moral specificity.

# Epistemic sparsity
(1) ⇒ in many if not most cases, we have insufficient - sometimes woefully insufficient - substance to form an accurate or viable 3rd-person model of ourselves in a given context. E.g. the thoughts of others about you, the way they weight these thoughts w.r.t. importance in their lives and decisions, the actual weightage of social norms, the way you actually appear to others, even the accuracy of your self-narrative w.r.t. the ground truth. The reason for such epistemic sparsity in many cases, I think, is simply the fact that we live natively in the 1st-person, a drastically different paradigm. Another reason is the opacity of other people's interiority. Yet another reason is our own biases (e.g. incentive bias, bias toward our own significance, bias toward projecting our interiority to others, etc.). On top of all this, we are fallible creatures often subject to all sorts of intellectual and logical fallacies.

# Moral specificity
(2) ⇒ the value of the SRL and its outcomes + the efforts that go into it (not to mention the taking up of our limited cognitive/psychological "space") is NOT a given. Not only is there nothing inherent in the SRL that makes it valuable by default, but also, there are many instances in which the SRL is pointless or actively detrimental (e.g. social anxiety, rumination, self-image over genuine engagement, manoeuvering leading to an environment of pretension and distrust, etc.). Indeed, if the 1st-person engagement with existence is where value stems from, the SRL would be suspect by default, rather than valuable by default.

# Conclusion
The upshot is this: (to put it somewhat figuratively) the SRL is guilty until proven innocent. More precisely, it is a framework and tool with real but specific value, definitively NOT universally valuable and of questionable value in general. Take it as such.
