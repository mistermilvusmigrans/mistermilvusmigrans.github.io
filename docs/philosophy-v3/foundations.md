# Provenance of value
I posit that, ultimately and fundamentally, value arises from something unchosen, something that is tied to or emerges from the core/given nature of a goal-driven being. Now, what this foundation of value is, is to me an open question. In my experience, based on how I have seen myself and others think, act, and orient themselves through life, I think this foundation may not be universal or even shared among the human species.

Regardless of what this foundation is, though, the above view on value means that values - and consequently morality - are not universal nor objective in some neutral perspective (if such can exist). Instead, value is inextricably tied to the individual, first-person frame of reference. Moral judgement, thus, is always personal, an evaluation of how something/someone relates to your personal goals, interests, desires, fears, etc.

This does not mean "anything goes". There is no objective basis by which personally-motivated judgement is inherently "lesser" or more negotiable than some impersonal framework. Think of it this way: your care and prioritisation of your loved ones is probably intensely personal, yet you would not regard this as "just personal preference" or casual or unimportant. Likewise, I don't hold my moral convictions as "just preference" or negotiable, and may even fight for them if needed. I'm just recognising their source.


# Meaning and worth
I'm in a strange position where I find life meaningless at scale and invaluable in detail. If value is not inherent in reality as such but tied to a personal frame of reference, an impersonal/abstract view of "life as a whole" or "life as such" yields no worth or meaning. To me, milestones, achievement, plans, ambitions, goals, longevity itself, the future, legacy ... all these only matter insofar as they tie into the details of my life as I live and experience it. For example, when I write on a social issue and advocate a position, when I stand up for myself or others, when I push myself for a project ... what any sense of "worth" really stems from is the engagement, focus, hope, excitement, compulsion/obsession, experience... as lived in the moment, each moment an end in itself. Without purpose, there is little to no motivation in the moment, but thus, you see, I seek purpose to generate and experience this motivation. Without constructive, long-term and principled action, purpose and motivation deteriorate, but you see, to me, these constructive, long-term and principled actions are not ends in themselves but a means to generate a lived reality I am inclined and motivated to engage with. It is almost paradoxical how the "ends" in a timeline are really a means to life in the moment. My destination is for the journey as much as my journey is for the destination, in different respects, inextricably tied to each other in both ways at once.

But let me be very clear. Life as a whole, life as such, life at scale...  these are still meaningless to me. My values are still fundamentally the emergent property of my core composition and structure. I don't feel special or optimistic about life as a whole or driven to "make something of my life" in any overarching way. I don't expect things to "work out in the end", I don't expect to "live a life worth living" or die fulfilled. I don't feel any moral obligation or inherent value for my life - life is optional, its value is contingent on my experience of it.

# Key attributes of value
## Individuation in value
Even if value is constructed/formed/absorbed interpersonally or socially or through any other non-first-person frame (e.g. biology, evolution, etc.), the actual process of experiencing or creating worth is individual, because we are an individuated species, not instinctually eusocial or mechanistic. This is how consciousness is instantiated in me, and in humans in general.

## How values project into the future
Given the above-described individuated nature of valuing - that the locus of valuing is a single site, not a distributed one - it follows that the frame of reference for valuing is not discretised/fragmented across time. The future is a potential of the present, not a frame of reference that exists on its own or that carries a separate moral weight. Value always lives in the present, even when aimed in some way toward the future. Values are emergent, not eternal.

##  Values w.r.t. the shape of life
What values emerge from life depends on what "life" is for the living being in question. Based on my observation, life as concretely lived isn't universalisable beyond some basic, broad mechanisms. A tree, an ant, a scientist, a mystic, all live/may live life starting from different positions about what it implies in their contexts.

# The goal of goals
Pages of proof and derivation amount to a few lines of conclusion. Neither holds its weight without the other. Like a whole apparatus of bloodstreams, neurons, and flesh that amounts to the bare experience of existence. When you take the journey, heed the destination, tied to the journey that becomes an aspect of the destination itself. When you read or revise, heed the intent, desire, vision. To master how to make a table you must master the steps and principles it takes. The act of journeying - of reading, revising, writing - consists of these steps, but they would mean nothing if you lose sight of the table.

# The instrumentality of virtue
I am not intelligent - I'm capable of applying certain forms of intelligence in certain areas and contexts. I can deepen and broaden my capabilities and abilities, but I think there are limits to that. I am not honest, not a person of integrity, not reliable, not benevolent, not kind... in the same manner. To me, these virtues - insofar as they ARE virtues - are instrumental, not identifying. I don't loathe myself. I don't love myself. I just am myself.

Now, the question is: instrumental to what? I don't know if I can answer this question in its entirety, but I can scope my answer to my life as it is now. Currently, I work long hours usually solving complex problems for others. I find my time and energy depleted with little to show for in terms of finances, health, capacity-building, engagement. Now, I think I have the components/material I need to build these, at least to some extent. Now, time and energy I can sort of carve out if I moderate my commitments (which could "cost" me in terms of my standing in my job, but I believe the value I'd get to be of a more desirable and sustainable kind). What remains, though, is clarity. In a sense, all that I've written so far and all that I'll write from hereon are for clarity's sake. But what I'm referring to is clarity about why to engage more intensively and extensively with the technical material and experience I've gathered. Why?

This is a question I've always struggled with in my life. I've often had the drive to engage in this way, almost obsessively, with work and/or study. But after the deadline, or after my initial impulse died down and I was left to contend with increasingly complex/advancing material whose meaning became less and less clear to me, my efforts often fizzled out, and I was left wondering if there had been any point to all this.

Now, let me cut to the chase. I am not looking for some special purpose or drive that'll turn my life and character around. My view of values precludes such grandiose narratives, I think. What I'm seeking is a mechanism by which I can sustain my life more reliably, because I happen to want to live, I fear hardship and deterioration in will, and I desire comfort and power (though not as permanent states but as tools in my grasp, to be used when I need). By seeking a "why", I'm seeking what I think is such a mechanism. The "why", ironically, is instrumental.

I have identified 2 axes of worth (if this is the right term) when pursuing a commitment to engage, learn and build: (1) engagement, (2) empowerment. Let me pull on these threads, both theoretically and concretely.

# Engagement and empowerment
In simple terms, engagement is the affirmation and appreciation of that which exists ("appreciation" here does not just mean the recognition of the positive (however you define "positive") but any kind of recognition of the facts and their implications with respect to one's context and goals), and empowerment is the creation of paths toward value/potential value ("creation" here refers to creation from the subject's own perspective, not from an omniscient 3rd person; hence, discovering such paths for oneself is also a form of empowerment, empowerment through knowledge, in this case). Empowerment is a form of engagement, and engagement is essentially the act of sustaining one's experience of existence; I say sustaining because I posit that, as a living being, key to sustaining one's experience of existence (and thereby one's life) is the affirmation of facts not just as an observer but as an active desirer/seeker (hence, affirmation with respect to one's context and goals). I even posit that facts are meaningless - literally, not just as a figure of speech - when one has no purpose (i.e. goals), because perception is differentiated from mechanistic markings/impressions by a consistent pattern of response (e.g. consistently orienting oneself toward one's survival), and  conception is only possible through selective focus, which in turn depends on some non-arbitrary standard by which one can decide what to select, which in turn relies (in my view) on purpose(s). In this light, empowerment is the form of engagement that reinforces that which makes engagement possible, which is why I hold it as a crucial form of engagement, especially considering my view of values.

Concretely, I would bring up these examples. Instead of focusing on "doing a good job" as the end, "doing a good job" would be a means to engagement (dealing with facts productively/constructively) and/or empowerment (e.g. building skills/capacities, building something that can add value to myself and/or others, sustaining myself and my mind through productive work and its results, etc.). Instead of focusing on "reading/learning something well" as the end, "reading/learning something well" would be a means to engagement (dealing with knowledge/ideas more deeply) and/or empowerment (e.g. gaining awareness about an area of life (e.g. economics, sociology), gaining insight/perspective (e.g. about the factors of prosperity and poverty, about how systemic harm manifests and perpetuates in subtle ways), being able to build on these knowledge and ideas toward goals such as (a) envisioning more effective social systems or (b) identifying, questioning, and distancing myself from harmful beliefs/practices). Instead of focusing on "being a good friend" as the end, "being a good friend" would be a means to engagement (engaging with the presence and potential of someone you value) and/or empowerment (e.g. furthering the wellbeing of someone whose presence matters to you practically and psychologically, absorbing lessons (explicit or implicit), aligning yourself with them toward long-term progress (e.g. as a part of a project, a joint effort, etc.)).
